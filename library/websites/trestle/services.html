IT services, written as jobs we take.

Six practices. Each one has a named lead, a typical shape of work, and a way we stay after delivery.

01

Custom software

We build the desks and products your people already work in: internal tools, customer applications, integrations, and the APIs between them. The brief is the work, not a catalogue of modules.

Typical jobs: operations desks, member or patient portals, mobile field tools, and the joins between a ledger you must keep and a system you are allowed to change.

  • Discovery that sits the floor before anyone opens an editor
  • Two-week cuts with a standing review
  • Handover of source, runbooks, and the first month of live support

Request this service

Recent

Saltpan teller desk

Replaced a three-system queue with one desk. Counter time down a third. The same four engineers sat the first six weeks live.

02

Cloud and infrastructure

Tenancy, migration, and the unglamorous racks. We plan the night, move the load, and watch the first week. We do not name a vendor and call it a strategy.

Typical jobs: leaving a single room of machines, splitting environments, and putting a written rollback next to the cutover note.

  • Inventory before architecture
  • Rehearsed cutovers with a named rollback
  • Cost and capacity reviewed after thirty days, not in the sales deck

Request this service

Recent

Loom warehouse move

Three racks left on metal, the rest in a tenant we run. Zero sales-hour downtime. Watch stayed on our desk for ninety days.

03

Cybersecurity

Assessment, hardening, monitoring, and a person who answers when the watch fires. Built for desks that cannot go dark, not for a certificate on a slide.

  • Threat walk of the actual estate, not a generic list
  • Identity, backup, and the paths an attacker already likes
  • A written hour-one response, rehearsed twice a year

Request this service

Recent

Ward 7 night watch

Sealed tenant, named on-call, and a board the floor can read. Two incidents in a year; both contained inside the hour-one note.

04

IT consulting

A written map before a line of code: what to keep, what to cut, who owns the call when it fails. We will tell you not to build.

  • Current-state inventory you can keep after we leave
  • A dated roadmap with owners, not themes
  • Architecture that names the ugly constraint

Request this service

Recent

Eastwick board pack

Six systems, one recommended cut, two deferred. The deferred work is still deferred — on purpose.

05

Data and analytics

The numbers your floor already knows, drawn so a shift lead can read them without a briefing. Warehouses, reports, and the joins nobody wanted to own.

  • One definition of a number, written down
  • Reports that match the shift, not the slide
  • Access rules that survive a new hire

Request this service

Recent

Holdfast week pack

One weekly pack replaced four exports. The product lead stopped asking for a “real number.”

06

Managed support

A named person on the line, every hour. Patches, watches, and the call when a box goes quiet. The crew that built it is allowed to keep it.

  • Named on-call, not a shared inbox
  • Median first response measured, published to you monthly
  • Changes written in the same book as incidents
