The Night-Wind on the Ghyll

COMPILATION OF 1845 • PENNED AT WUTHERING CRAGS
In summer’s mellow silent sphere,
A breath across the heather crept,
And whispered in my dreaming ear
Of sorrow while the hamlet slept.
The moorland gales are roaring loud,
They shake the lattice, cold and bare,
While through the rent and rushing cloud
The pale moon casts her frozen glare.
Lie down; our bed is dark and steep,
And lonely under desolate stone;
Yet wild winds rock the dead to sleep,
Where none can weep, and none can mourn.
