01 — Logo

Symbol, wordmark, lockup

The symbol is two overlapping rings cut by a horizontal vesica: an eye, an orbit and a spark in one. The star sits on the optical centre. Use the horizontal lockup by default; the symbol alone works for small or square spaces.

02 — Colour

Ink, cream and one light

Omnia is a near-black and warm-cream brand. Amber is light, not paint. It shows up as a glow that fades into ink or cream and rarely as a flat fill. Contrast ratios below are computed live from the tokens (WCAG 2.2).

03 — Typography

A quiet grotesk and a mono

04 — Light & gradients

Amber is a light source

Gradients are radial glows that come from one edge or corner and fade into ink or cream. Use one light per surface. Never put type over the hottest part of the glow, and never fill the symbol with a gradient.

Horizon · edge light into graphiteKai Ørensen

① The light enters from an edge, usually the left or top-left, and never sits dead centre on ink.

② The hot core (#e8a800 to #f0b400) takes up at most 20% of the surface, and the falloff stays long and soft.

③ The line-art device (dashed or dotted symbol at 120–160% scale) can sit on top of the glow at 30–45% opacity.

05 — Line-art device

The symbol as a field

The symbol's outline, drawn at a huge scale and cropped by the format, turns into a texture. Dashed on stationery, dotted on posters, solid hairline on screens.
